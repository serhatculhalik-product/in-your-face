## Design Specificity Verdict

Functionally authored, visually coherent at rest, under-authored at the seams. The protection vocabulary, recovery model, escalation ladder, and explicit control could only belong to this product. The menu-bar surface is the clearest expression of the Quiet Sentinel. The callback receipt, readiness exit semantics, context-dependent controls, Activity pane, and Strong Alert action ladder weaken the sense that one system owns the entire journey.

The deterministic scan returned zero findings only because the detector supports web file types and found zero scannable Swift files. It was run once on `Sources/InYourFace` and returned `[]`; this is an unsupported-language false-negative, not a clean bill of health. Manual screenshot and source evidence was used instead. No browser overlay exists because PNGs and SwiftUI/AppKit are not DOM-bearing targets.

## Overall Impression

Preserve the native macOS identity, semantic typography, system controls, explicit state truth, destructive-action safeguards, menu-bar calmness, and single accessibility tree across displays. The biggest opportunity is to make every transition and urgent decision instantly truthful: what is protected, what remains uncommitted, what happens next, and which action is primary.

Apple calibration: the Settings toolbar, pane titles, standard controls, SF Symbols, default actions, and native confirmation patterns are aligned with macOS conventions. The product's app-initiated cross-display reminder windows are an intentional exception to Apple's general preference for user-controlled full screen and system notifications; this critique does not reopen that accepted requirement. Within that exception, chrome, focus, action hierarchy, keyboard access, and accessibility must be especially predictable.

## Surface critique

### 01 — Connect Google Calendar

Clear Step 1 of 2, one default action, credible read-only/encryption copy, and stable footer. The content is top-heavy with a large dead zone, while the setup consequence is relegated to tiny footer copy. Fit the step more closely to content and reduce repetition without weakening the privacy promise.

### 02 — OAuth return

The raw `127.0.0.1` URL and one-line text/plain receipt are the largest product-boundary break. It does not distinguish authorization received from account connection completed and looks like development plumbing at the highest-trust moment. The app should own a tiny truthful handoff state, with actual connection success confirmed inside Meeting Incoming.

### 03 — Choose Monitored Calendars

Strong native scaffolding: account context, search, selection count, checkbox list, and guarded default action. The expected untouched state uses the same orange fault grammar as stale/unavailable coverage, and only part of the 14-item list is visible without a strong continuation cue.

### 04 — Remove from protection

This is good. The native confirmation names the object, states the immediate consequence, uses destructive/cancel roles, and leaves a safe exit. Do not replace it with a custom modal.

### 05 — Protection ready

The success statement, account count, defaults, and finishing action are sound. Three issues collide: the success peak becomes a dense mini-Settings pane; account-to-calendar mapping is ambiguous when both look like email addresses; and identical global preferences render as checkboxes here but switches in Settings. More seriously, readiness choices are drafts committed only by Finish Setup, while native Close remains available and the page already says Protection ready.

### 06 — Menu bar

The best surface: calm, compact, explicit, redundant status, one immediate operational control, and a restrained utility footer. The fixed width visibly truncates `Quit Meeting Incom…`; this predicts worse localization failures. The richer window-style extra is justified by live status and repair/restore actions, despite Apple's menu-default guidance.

### 07 — Accounts

Native toolbar navigation, grouped Form, named per-account coverage, and correct Disconnect/Remove distinction are strong. Account rows, device-bound security explanation, external access management, add-account action, and Availability compete inside one long composition; Availability starts below the supplied viewport.

### 08 — Calendars

The shared selector is a strong reusable component. In the settled state, the gray confirmation label is weaker than the disabled blue-tinted CTA, making completion look like a blocked task. Pending-copy also needs to distinguish newly selected calendars from calendars that remain protected.

### 09 — Reminders

This is the cleanest Settings pane and a useful baseline. However, the same Early Reminder and Blocking Mode controls change from checkboxes in readiness to switches here because Toggle style is inherited from the container. The conditional banner says protection is off because of timing changes, but its condition is pending calendar confirmation, so its cause and scope can be wrong.

### 10 — Protection Activity

The day boundary, filter, timestamps, actor distinction, and native list are sound. This pane begins on a different inset/compositional grid than the other Settings panes. `calendar · account` choices become nearly indistinguishable when both are email addresses, and rows repeat actor, title, sentence, account, and calendar metadata beyond what scanning needs.

### 11 — Early Reminder

Calm and understandable, with a correct native Close, bounded description, Snooze, and explicit consequence. The full date outranks the more urgent countdown; the visual path jumps centered → left → centered; the surface name repeats in title bar and body; and two consequence paragraphs separate controls from their meaning.

### 12 — Early Reminder, Snooze open

The native four-choice menu is correct. Its temporary overlap is standard macOS behavior, not a systemic defect. Move supporting copy so the expected overlay does not hide the explanation it belongs to.

### 13 — Strong Alert, starting now

The title/timing and unavailable native Close are correct. Five top-level outcomes appear as a narrow vertical ladder: Join, I joined another way, Stop reminders, Got it, Pause All Protection. The defining action has too little physical magnitude, consequence copy is tiny, and raw provenance competes with urgency. The inactive gray Join state should be verified, not custom-colored away.

### 14 — Strong Alert, overdue/Pause open

Overdue timing is clear and active accent makes Join legible. The Pause menu is bounded and separated, but expands three more choices inside an already overloaded moment. A subtle material shell inside the rounded hidden-title window produces a double-boundary effect. Preserve every action and the no-close behavior; reorganize hierarchy rather than deleting capability.

## Whole-system findings

- Information architecture is sound: menu bar for glance/action, onboarding for activation, Settings for ongoing configuration, alerts for intervention. Readiness is the exception because it exposes result, account expansion, four preference areas, and completion at equal depth.
- Density bands are legitimate: compact menu bar, standard Settings, generous alerts. Harmful inconsistency occurs inside the Settings window and inside the alert family, not merely because different surfaces use different spacing.
- Typography is strongly native: 105 semantic font modifiers and no custom or fixed-point font system were found. Temporal hierarchy still needs correction in Early Reminder.
- Color is sparse and system-based, but orange is overloaded across no coverage, stale, reconnect, unavailable, unverified, and conflict-primary states. Text and symbols prevent a color-only failure, but rapid recognition suffers.
- Button semantics and destructive roles are strong. Physical hierarchy is weakest in Strong Alert and in the settled Calendars footer.
- Native radii and containers are mostly appropriate. Do not create a universal card kit. The Strong Alert's nested material boundary is the one obvious custom-shell outlier.
- SF Symbols are consistent; their semantic mapping is not. Distinct recovery states should not all use the same filled warning triangle.
- Settings navigation matches Apple's toolbar-pane model. The content grids and scroll ownership do not feel shared across Accounts, Calendars, Reminders, and Activity.
- Form controls are native, but identical shared Toggles inherit different visual types across containers. Use switches for global feature settings and checkboxes for multi-selection, consistently.
- Empty and loading states use ContentUnavailableView and labeled ProgressView well. Error emphasis varies between red, primary, and secondary treatment; a shared inline feedback grammar is missing.
- Destructive actions are a strength: calendar deselection, Disconnect, Remove Account, and local-data reset have explicit consequences and safe cancellation.
- Copy is unusually precise, but alert paragraphs become exhaustive and provenance reads like diagnostics. `Activity` versus `Protection Activity` is a minor naming drift.
- Post-action behavior is generally visible through state changes and accessibility announcements. The readiness draft/commit boundary is not visible enough.
- Accessibility structure is strong in source: semantic controls, default shortcuts, focusable headings, Reduce Motion handling, action hints, and one accessibility tree across replicated displays. Dark mode is the only supplied visual state; light, Increase Contrast, Reduce Transparency, focus, VoiceOver, and localization remain validation gaps.

## Cognitive load and emotional journey

Moderate overall, with three checklist failures concentrated in readiness and Strong Alert: single focus, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure. Readiness exposes seven decisions/actions; Strong Alert exposes five before the Pause submenu. Calendar selection has many items but is adequately scaffolded by search, checkboxes, and a live count.

The journey starts with credible privacy reassurance, falls into a raw localhost trust valley, recovers through a competent calendar selector, dilutes its success peak with dense configuration, reaches its emotional high point in the calm menu bar, then asks for too much deliberation in the defining urgent alert.

## What is working

1. Protection truth is treated as first-class UI: status is named, symbolized, and explained rather than inferred.
2. Consequential actions are unusually well protected with accurate scope, native roles, confirmation, and safe cancellation.
3. Native and accessible behavior is structural: system type/controls, adaptive content, keyboard defaults, VoiceOver hints, Reduce Motion, and one accessible interaction tree across displays.

## Priority issues

### [P0] Readiness can imply completion while leaving staged choices uncommitted

Why it matters: a user can read Protection ready, see Start at Login switched on, and close the native window. Only Finish Setup invokes the commit path. If the final choices are not applied, the app may not launch later when the user expects protection, which directly threatens the product promise.

Fix: intercept red Close and Command-W on readiness. Present native choices using existing semantics: Continue Setup, Finish Later (apply choices and keep setup resumable), and Finish Setup (apply and complete). No close path may silently discard a staged choice. Also state near the controls that choices apply when setup is finished.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] OAuth completion is an unowned, ambiguous trust transition

Why it matters: it looks unfinished and does not truthfully distinguish authorization received from account connected. First-timers may retry or abandon.

Fix: serve a minimal product-owned authorization-received page and bring the app forward; confirm Connected only in-app after token exchange succeeds.

Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard`

### [P1] Shared semantics change appearance and meaning across contexts

Why it matters: identical settings become checkbox or switch, expected No Coverage looks like an error, four recovery states share one icon/color, and a Reminders banner misidentifies calendar confirmation as timing changes. Learned behavior does not transfer.

Fix: establish and implement a small shared grammar for global toggles, protection/coverage states, and pending/confirmed action regions.

Suggested command: `$impeccable document`

### [P1] Strong Alert flattens five outcomes at the moment of maximum urgency

Why it matters: users nearing or past start time must read and compare instead of acting. The product's core value becomes deliberation.

Fix: retain all actions but group by outcome, make Join/Choose Link spatially dominant and the sole accent action, shorten repeat copy, and separate occurrence actions from global Pause. Share the action structure between normal and conflict variants.

Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Settings and provenance lack a shared composition and scanning grammar

Why it matters: Activity appears to belong to a different app pane; Accounts is over-grouped; long email-like calendar/account strings repeat in filters and alerts; completed states are visually weaker than disabled actions.

Fix: create one Settings pane scaffold, one provenance formatter, and one confirmed/pending status region, then migrate panes individually.

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`

## Prioritized implementation plan

Legend: A = system-wide design change, B = shared-component change, C = individual-screen change.

### UX-001 — Make readiness exit explicit and safe

- Priority/type: P0 · C
- Affected: 05 Protection ready; onboarding native Close/Command-W
- Problem: the headline and controls imply ready/applied state, but only Finish Setup commits drafts.
- Why: Start at Login or reminder choices can be left unapplied while the user believes protection is complete.
- Direction: intercept close with Continue Setup, Finish Later, and Finish Setup using existing semantics; expose staged/applied state in copy.
- Scope: local behavior, governed by a global rule that staged changes never disappear silently.
- Dependencies: none.
- Acceptance: change each readiness preference, close via red traffic light and Command-W, and verify every exit is explicit; Finish Later persists values and keeps setup resumable; Finish Setup persists and completes; Continue returns without mutation; no path silently drops a draft.

### UX-002 — Give global settings one control grammar

- Priority/type: P1 · A+B
- Affected: 05 readiness, 09 Reminders
- Problem: the same Early Reminder and Blocking Mode toggles are checkboxes in readiness and switches in Settings; readiness mixes those with a Start at Login switch.
- Why: identical semantics look like different kinds of choice.
- Direction: use switches for global feature settings in both contexts; reserve checkboxes for calendar multi-selection. Normalize row alignment while retaining native sizing.
- Scope: global rule implemented in shared reminder controls.
- Dependencies: none.
- Acceptance: the three global booleans in readiness use one switch language; Early Reminder and Blocking Mode match Settings in role/state; calendar selection remains checkbox-based; keyboard and VoiceOver expose unchanged names and values.

### UX-003 — Unify protection and coverage state semantics

- Priority/type: P1 · A+B
- Affected: 03, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, unverified/conflict alert states
- Problem: No Coverage, Stale, Reconnect Required, and Unavailable share orange/triangle treatment while settings summary and menu bar use separate mappings; orange also marks unrelated uncertainty/primary states.
- Why: users cannot distinguish expected incompleteness from failure at a glance.
- Direction: define one text + SF Symbol + tone matrix by state/scope; keep color redundant and accent reserved for actions.
- Scope: global, implemented through shared presentation data/components.
- Dependencies: none.
- Acceptance: the same state has the same icon/tone everywhere; expected first-run No Coverage is visibly calmer than stale/unavailable; reconnect is account-specific; every state remains legible in grayscale and Increase Contrast; no passive state uses action accent.

### UX-004 — Standardize provenance formatting

- Priority/type: P2 · A+B
- Affected: 05, 10, 13, 14
- Problem: unlabeled `calendar · account` strings become ambiguous and repeat.
- Why: implementation details compete with task information and duplicate names are hard to decode.
- Direction: create a shared formatter/presentation model that labels or groups account/calendar identity, de-duplicates repetition, and offers compact/standard/urgent variants.
- Scope: global/shared.
- Dependencies: UX-003.
- Acceptance: same-named calendars across two accounts are distinguishable without parsing suffixes; urgent alerts use a concise summary; full provenance remains available and readable by VoiceOver.

### UX-005 — Establish one Settings pane scaffold

- Priority/type: P1 · A+B
- Affected: 07–10
- Problem: Accounts/Reminders use padded grouped Forms; Calendars uses a custom VStack; Activity uses another full-width composition and different leading grid.
- Why: pane switching feels like switching products and essential content can start below the default viewport.
- Direction: standardize content insets, maximum readable width, top alignment, scroll ownership, section rhythm, and bottom action behavior while preserving Form/List controls appropriate to each pane.
- Scope: global/shared; no universal card kit.
- Dependencies: none.
- Acceptance: toolbar and title remain native; screenshots of all four panes share a visible leading grid and section rhythm; each pane scrolls once; no critical state/action is clipped at default size; resizing remains native.

### UX-006 — Make pending versus confirmed protection a shared action region

- Priority/type: P1 · A+B
- Affected: 08 Calendars, 09 Reminders, menu/status summaries
- Problem: completed Calendars leaves a disabled blue CTA more salient than success; the Reminders banner claims timing changes triggered a calendar-confirmation state and can overstate that all protection is off.
- Why: users may think a settled task is blocked or misunderstand which calendars are protected.
- Direction: show an actionable region only when confirmation is pending, with accurate counts/scope; show a quiet settled confirmation otherwise. Use the same model wherever pending state appears.
- Scope: global/shared.
- Dependencies: UX-003, UX-005.
- Acceptance: confirmed state has no dominant disabled CTA; adding a calendar immediately shows an enabled confirmation and says existing confirmed calendars remain protected; navigating to Reminders names the real pending cause; changing reminder timing alone never produces calendar-confirmation copy.

### UX-007 — Build one semantic alert action region

- Priority/type: P1 · A+B
- Affected: 11–14 plus normal/conflict variants
- Problem: action roles, consequence placement, Pause menus, and shells are repeated in separate trees; Strong Alert exposes five peer outcomes.
- Why: drift accumulates and urgent decisions require parsing.
- Direction: shared layout roles for primary/default, handled alternative, occurrence-level stop/passive close, and global Pause; consequences sit beside their action; logical tab order; one accent action.
- Scope: global/shared.
- Dependencies: UX-003.
- Acceptance: normal and conflict alerts use the same action grammar; every existing action remains; Join/Choose Link is sole default/accent when available; Stop reminders remains confirmation-protected; Pause is visually separated; action order matches VoiceOver/tab order.

## Target design principles

1. Native first, exception-aware: use macOS components and behaviors everywhere the product's explicit interruption contract does not require an exception.
2. Truth at transitions: distinguish staged, pending, received, connected, protected, and complete.
3. Same meaning, same control: global settings use one control language; selection uses another; state visuals come from one semantic matrix.
4. Time before metadata: in alerts, commitment, temporal state, and next action outrank provenance and explanation.
5. One dominant action per decision context: retain alternatives but group and subordinate them.
6. Density follows responsibility: compact glance, standard configuration, generous intervention; internal grids stay consistent within a surface family.
7. Color reinforces; words and symbols explain.
8. Native adaptability is part of polish: key/inactive, light/dark, contrast/transparency, localization, keyboard, VoiceOver, and multi-display behavior are baseline.

## First recommended improvement

Implement UX-001, Make readiness exit explicit and safe. It is the only finding that can directly create a false sense of completed protection while staged settings remain unapplied, and it is narrow enough to implement, screenshot, test, accept, or revert independently.

## Minor observations and questions

- Do not treat Early Reminder traffic lights versus Strong Alert's no-close window as inconsistency; the behaviors are intentionally different.
- Do not treat native Snooze/Pause menu overlap as clipping; it is transient platform behavior.
- Do not standardize every radius or padding value; the existing density bands are legitimate.
- Verify that the Settings window restores the last pane as Apple recommends; this is not proven by screenshots.
- Ask whether every caption in an alert would still be needed if consequence were embedded in action grouping.
- Ask whether the callback is transport plumbing or the moment the product earns calendar trust; visually, it is currently both.
